POKEMON TCG N64 PORT - VISUAL / ART-DIRECTION REVIEW (baseline c17975fa)
=========================================================================
Reviewer: Claude Fable 5.1 (independent visual/design review, read-only).
Evidence: the 25 native RDP captures in port/reviews/n64-polish/before/
(contact-sheet.png plus each PNG at native 320x240 and 3-4x nearest zooms),
the two approved concepts in port/maps/prototypes/, the original GBC maps and
briefs in port/maps/, and the presentation source listed at the end.

What I could and could not verify
---------------------------------
* I read every PNG with image-capable Read; the images rendered correctly
  and I could resolve single texels on the zooms.
* NO final VI scanout images exist. The capture helper reported that ares
  window capture failed (cgWindowNotFound) and the main manifest now records
  this. Every observation below is from the RDP framebuffer BEFORE VI
  resample/antialias/dedither. I therefore do not comment on edge aliasing,
  dither noise or texture shimmer: those are exactly what VI changes. All
  composition, palette, value, lighting, texture-scale, camera and layout
  findings are unaffected by VI filtering and stand.
* Timing, input feel, animation cadence and transitions are inferred from
  code (presentation.c, dialogue.c, intro.c, main.c), not observed. Marked
  "inferred" where that matters. Physical-console performance is unverified.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. ONE-PARAGRAPH VERDICT
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The layouts, prop placement, cameras and story choreography are faithful and
the island is already close to the approved concept. What is missing is the
entire LIGHTING LAYER, and that is what separates these frames from a 1999
retail N64 game. Concretely: (a) every textured surface is exported with
pure white vertex colour, so floors and walls have no shading at all; (b)
there is no contact shadow under any actor or prop, so nothing is grounded;
(c) the side walls are a 2-tile wainscot strip stretched to full wall
height, producing huge horizontal stripes that dominate every interior;
(d) floors are the brightest element in most rooms (measured mean value
0.75-0.86), inverting the value hierarchy so characters and focal props read
weaker than the carpet; (e) the near south wall is a flat bright gold slab
across the bottom 10-15% of every room frame, and the top corners show the
navy void above 4-unit walls, so rooms read as "dollhouse in space". None of
these need new geometry or runtime lights. Almost all of it is a vertex-colour
bake in build_world.py plus one runtime octagon per actor, which both the N64
GL path and the WebGL path already consume (both multiply vColor x texture).
That is the first pass. The second pass is material/prop quality and UI.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. ART-DIRECTION TARGET (the look every room should converge on)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Name it "the lit showroom". The approved lightning_club_n64_v1.png already
encodes it:

  * Value hierarchy, brightest to darkest:
      1. characters (faces, shirts)           ~0.85-1.00
      2. the room's focal object (shrine, altar, pool, arena) ~0.80-0.95
      3. floor graphic along the central aisle   ~0.65-0.80
      4. floor at the walls / under props       ~0.40-0.55 (baked AO)
      5. wainscot and props                     ~0.55-0.75
      6. upper walls                            ~0.30-0.45, darkening upward
      7. the near (south) wall block            ~0.25-0.40 (it is a frame)
      8. ceiling/void                           near-black, warm not blue
  * Pools of light: the aisle and the focal object get a +10-20% vertex
    brightening; everything within ~2.5 units of a wall gets a smooth
    darkening to ~0.45. This single gradient is what makes the concept read
    as a room instead of a diagram.
  * Grounding: an opaque dark octagon (0.43 x 0.32 units, like the
    Lightning prototype's "Contact shadow" material) under every actor and
    every free-standing prop. No alpha blending; era-correct and 8 tris.
  * Saturation: keep the clubs' loud GBC hues (they are the brand), but
    only the aisle/focal graphic stays at full saturation and value.
    Walls, the near block and floor edges drop to ~70% value so the hue
    reads as "painted surface under light" rather than "unlit emissive".
  * Walls: three bands, as in the concept. Wainscot 0-1.6 units carries the
    source livery (checker, pinstripe, cream base). Upper wall 1.6-4.4 units
    is a plain painted colour with a vertical gradient. A cornice/frieze
    band at the top carries the source's decorated row. The upper wall
    should leave the frame at the top in long rooms; no void.
  * Materials: small, repeated, hand-tiled textures (32x32 RGBA16 now;
    64x64 CI4 later), bilinear. Variation comes from vertex colour, not
    from bigger textures.
  * Motion: 30 Hz grid walking is fine (it is the source). Add idle
    breathing so standing actors are not statues, and fade warps.
  * UI: one visual language for story/menu text (cream paper, blue ink,
    bevelled frame, drop-shadowed text) and a matching dark variant for
    the duel board. Replace the plain 8x8 debug font treatment with the
    same font plus a 1-pixel dark drop shadow everywhere it sits on 3D.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PRIORITISED FINDINGS WITH IMAGE EVIDENCE
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Severity: [A] changes the whole game's impression, [B] per-room / per-screen
quality, [C] polish. Each item names the file to change in section 3.

[A1] No lighting on any textured surface.
  Evidence: every interior; clearest in map-12.png (Water), map-21.png
  (Psychic), map-33.png (Hall of Honor) where the floor is a uniform glare
  edge to edge. Measured mean value (HSV V) of the frame: Water 0.86,
  Psychic 0.84, Fire 0.84, Hall of Honor 0.82, Lobby 0.82, Science 0.79,
  Rock 0.78; the approved Lightning capture is 0.56 and the concept images
  sit around 0.5-0.6.
  Cause: build_world.py Scene.box() sets c=WHITE whenever a texture is
  present; Scene.floor() and walls() emit textured quads with the default
  WHITE. Only untextured prop faces receive the fixed per-face table
  (.6,.78,.86,1,.72,1). The overworld (author_overworld.py) DOES bake a
  sun term (.72+.28*N.dot(SUN)) and it shows: the island is the best frame.
  Fix: a vertex-colour post-pass over every room batch (section 3.1).

[A2] No contact shadows under actors; props float.
  Evidence: zoom map-15-player.png (Mark on the bolt, no shadow at all),
  map-01-lab.png (Sam, Mason and techs hover over cyan tiles), map-18-grass
  (topiaries and boulders float on the lawn), map-09-rock (gold statues
  float). The naming screen (naming-mark.png) DOES draw a blue ellipse under
  Mark and immediately reads as a finished screen. The Lightning prototype
  build_scene.py had opaque octagonal contact shadows; the finished
  character exports (model 7+) do not carry them, so they were lost when
  the detailed models replaced the fallbacks.
  Fix: runtime octagon per actor in scene.c, baked octagons under props in
  build_world.py (section 3.2, 3.1).

[A3] Side walls are a stretched 2-tile strip.
  Evidence: zooms map-27-wall.png, map-12-wall.png, map-24-wall.png: four
  to six broad horizontal bands of the livery colour sweeping along the
  full wall height. In map-21 (Psychic) and map-06 (Fighting) the bands
  are the loudest element in frame.
  Cause: walls() samples wall_tex=img.crop(16,(h-3)*8,32,16,...) (the
  2-row south band) and maps it with uv (0,1)..(span/2,0) over the whole
  4-unit wall, so a 2-tile-tall wainscot is magnified 2x vertically and the
  room's bright livery occupies the entire wall.
  Fix: three-band wall (section 3.3). This is faithful: 3D_GUIDE.md says
  the livery is a 2-tile wainscot under a plain wall.

[A4] Value hierarchy inverted: floors brightest, near block brightest.
  Evidence: map-12, map-21, map-33, map-14: the floor and the gold-capped
  south block at the bottom of frame are the brightest pixels; NPCs are
  darker than the carpet. In the Lightning concept the near blocks are
  mid-value gold on dark wood and the floor is deep indigo.
  Cause: south wall = brown box .9 high + GOLD cap at top shade 1.0,
  nearest to camera; floor textures at full source value with white vertex
  colour.
  Fix: per-room floor tint table + near-block darkening in the bake
  (3.1), and optionally drop the camera target so the block is thinner.

[A5] Void above the walls.
  Evidence: top corners of map-01, map-09, map-12, map-24, map-27 show the
  navy clear colour (.02,.025,.055) above the 4-unit wall. Reads as a set
  in a black studio. The Lightning concept's walls run out of frame.
  Fix: a ceiling slab at wall height with a dark warm vertex gradient, or
  walls to 5.5 units with the cornice band, plus a matching clear colour
  (3.1, 3.3).

[B1] Floors: scale, grout and tint.
  * Club lobby (map-14, zoom map-14-lobby.png): #deffb5 tiles with #00ff31
    grout; the 1-texel neon grout bilinear-spreads to ~3 px and the room
    becomes a green lightbox. This is the most garish floor in the game and
    it is the room every club shares.
  * Mason lab (map-01, mason-dialogue): saturated cyan tile grid reads as a
    swimming pool, especially under the cream dialogue panel.
  * Psychic (map-21): bright yellow tile floor in a room whose identity is
    violet; the floor should be lavender-cream at ~0.7 value.
  * Hall of Honor (map-33): glaring orange-yellow tiles; this is the one
    room that should be dim with a single pool of light on the altar.
  Fix: FLOOR_TINT per room multiplied into the floor vertex colours (zero
  texture work), and for lobby/foyer/lab a repainted 32x32 tile texture
  with grout at ~0.8 of tile value (3.1, 3.4).

[B2] Props are unlit saturated primitives.
  Evidence: map-18-grass (pure green balls in brown pots, lime lawn, grey
  boulders), map-27 (brazier = green cylinder + red ball), map-21 (torch =
  black pole + purple ball), map-09 (statue = gold cylinder + ball),
  map-06 (weights rack = row of black pegs), map-12 (benches and "horse"
  = white boxes). The per-face shade table gives them form, but at pure
  #ff0000 / #42d600 base colours they read as toy plastic.
  Fix: desaturate/darken prop base palette ~15-25%, add bottom-to-top
  vertex gradients on cylinder/ball, baked shadow octagons, and
  re-model the four worst props (3.1, 3.5).

[B3] Sam's table in Mason Lab is a 2.4-unit white block.
  Evidence: map-01-lab.png and sam-table.png: a large cream slab with the
  green mat and magenta balloons below it. The practice duel is the
  story's first set piece and the camera frames this block.
  Cause: scene_specs 'mason_laboratory' P('machine',8,14,5,6,2.4,3) draws
  the 'machine' recipe (brown box with white top cap). The source draws a
  duel table with a green playmat.
  Fix: use the 'table' recipe (legs + thin top carrying the source mat
  texture) at height ~0.85 like the lobby's duel table (3.5).

[B4] Water Club pool is a flat blue rectangle.
  Evidence: map-12-pool.png: single blue with a white border and no
  surface detail; palms and benches good.
  Fix: recess the pool 0.3 units with inner walls, tile the island's water
  texture with GL_REPEAT, bake darker vertex colour at the rim (3.5).

[B5] Overworld (map-00) vs concept: good, but dim and shadowless.
  Evidence: map-00-island.png. Terrain, cliffs, conifers, buildings and
  routes match overworld_n64_concept_v1.png. Gaps: the sea is a dark
  indigo (frame mean value 0.52) where the concept is cobalt with foam;
  buildings and trees cast nothing; the Poke Ball cursor has no shadow;
  the location label is a navy duel-UI box in a cream-paper story game.
  Fix: section 3.6 and 3.8.

[B6] Duel board is functional, not finished.
  Evidence: duel.png, duel-action-menu.png, duel-coin.png: flat
  #103028/#182048 fields, 56x30 bench rectangles with 6-character names,
  8x8 font with no shadow, text box and HUD share the same flat 2 px
  rectangle style. The card art thumbnails (64 px) are the only thing that
  reads as "Pokemon". The action menu and coin popup overlap the active
  Pokemon name (duel-coin.png).
  Fix: section 3.7 (second pass; the board is correct and legible today).

[B7] Three UI languages.
  Story panels: cream #f7f7de paper, blue #183050 ink, #4a739c edge.
  Duel: dark teal/navy. Island label: navy. Intro/title: GBC pixel art at
  1.5x point-sampled. Menus (n64_menu): same dark style as duel.
  Fix: one language (3.8).

[C1] Intro/title 1.5x nearest scaling.
  Evidence: title.png, intro-charizard.png: 160x144 art scaled 1.5x with
  FILTER_POINT gives alternating 1 px / 2 px columns (visible in the logo
  outline and the "TM"). Era-correct answer is bilinear for the blit (3.9).

[C2] Transitions (inferred from code).
  Warps are an instant cut (load_scene with no fade). Duel shutters are a
  linear 12-frame close from top and bottom; no hold on black, no sound
  sync. Camera cuts are instant on map change (correct) and eased at .24
  per frame in the Mason cinematic (good). Fix in 3.10.

[C3] Idle actors are statues (inferred from code).
  step_actor zeroes gait/bob/lean when not moving. Gait itself is good:
  distance-tied phase (one cycle per two tiles), contralateral arm swing
  (parts 2 and 3 negated), 6 degree run lean. Add an idle breath (3.10).

[C4] Camera uniformity.
  All 33 interiors use one recipe (eye y=max(7,h*.28125) at z=h+2, target
  z=h*.67). It is a sound "approved proportions" rule, and I would keep the
  Lightning camera untouched. But short rooms (foyers, Fighting Club, Dome
  entrance) at fov 64 show the most void and the most near-block, and the
  Hall of Honor would benefit from a lower, closer, more reverent framing.
  Treat as optional per-room overrides after A1-A5 land (3.11).

------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. IMPLEMENTATION SUGGESTIONS, PER FILE
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Design principle for all of it: PRESERVE geometry, UVs, indices and pivots.
Everything in 3.1 is a colour-only post-pass over the existing batches, so
the approved Lightning room (copied verbatim by copy_lightning()) and the
finished character exports are untouched, and the WebGL frontend picks up
the same look for free because world3d.js already computes
vColor * texture.

3.1 port/world3d/tools/build_world.py - lighting bake post-pass
  Add `bake_lighting(scene, width, height, props, slug)` and call it once
  per interior after walls()/prop() and also on the `lightning` scene
  returned by copy_lightning() (its batches are plain vertex lists, so the
  pass applies unchanged). For every vertex (x,y,z,u,v,r,g,b,a) in every
  batch with model==0 compute a multiplier m and replace rgb with
  rgb*m (clamped). Suggested terms, all cheap and tunable:

    # 1. floor AO toward walls: d = distance to nearest wall inner face
    wall_ao  = 0.45 + 0.55*smoothstep(0, 2.5, d)       # floor verts (y<0.05)
    # 2. floor AO toward prop footprints: dp = distance to nearest prop rect
    prop_ao  = 0.6 + 0.4*smoothstep(0, 0.9, dp)
    # 3. aisle / focal pool of light (per room: a rect list in scene_specs)
    pool     = 1.0 + 0.18*smoothstep(1.5, 0, distance_to_pool_rect)
    # 4. wall vertical gradient (wall verts: y>0.05, within 1.2 of a wall)
    wall_v   = 0.85 - 0.45*clamp((y-1.6)/2.8, 0, 1)     # 0.85 at wainscot top -> 0.40 at top
    # 5. near (south) block: everything with z > height-2.2
    near     = 0.40
    # 6. prop bottom darkening (prop verts: 0<y<prop.height)
    prop_v   = 0.75 + 0.25*clamp(y/ (0.6*prop.height), 0, 1)

  Floor quads are 4x4 tiles, so the AO gradient will be coarse. For the
  three tile rows nearest each wall, emit 2x2-tile quads instead (change
  the `range(2,w-2,4)` loops in Scene.floor() to step 2 when within 4 tiles
  of a wall). Cost: roughly +40% floor quads, which is a small share of
  room triangles (floors are ~100-200 tris; Grass Club's 4016 tris are
  topiaries).

  Add FLOOR_TINT (rgb multipliers) keyed by slug; first values to try:
    club_lobby      (0.78,0.82,0.74)   kills the neon grout
    club_foyer      (0.82,0.84,0.78)
    mason_laboratory(0.80,0.86,0.92)   cyan -> cool grey-blue
    psychic_club    (0.78,0.70,0.88)   yellow tile -> lavender cream
    hall_of_honor   (0.70,0.62,0.50)   dim gold, then the altar pool +0.25
    water_club      (0.84,0.84,0.76)
    fire_club       (0.86,0.78,0.70)
    everything else (0.85,0.85,0.85)
  Keep the Lightning floor at 1.0 (it is the reference) but still apply
  wall AO and pool terms so it matches the concept's pools of light.

  Prop shadows: in prop(), after each free-standing recipe (palm, topiary,
  shrub, flowers, boulder, statue, serpent, horse, brazier, torch, balloon,
  chair, bench, weight, ball, pillar) emit an octagon at y=0.012,
  radius ~ (w*0.45, depth*0.4), colour rgb(16,10,28) (indigo rooms) or a
  per-room shadow tint = floor_base*0.25. Opaque, no texture, 6 tris.
  The engine already draws untextured polygons in the same batch key
  space, so no renderer change.

  Ceiling / void: emit one quad at y=wall_height over the walkable rect
  with colour (26,18,14) and the four wall-top rows of vertices given
  wall_v=0.40 so wall meets ceiling without a hard edge. Then set
  glClearColor in main.c to the same (26,18,14)/255. The ceiling costs 2
  tris and removes finding A5 in every room. (Alternative: wall height 5.5
  with cornice; both are fine, ceiling is cheaper and safer for the
  near-plane.)

  Prop palette: in the colour constants, replace pure primaries with
  value-controlled versions, e.g. RED (255,0,0)->(214,40,36),
  GREEN (0,255,49)->(62,170,70), GOLD->(214,172,52), DARKGREEN->(20,96,40),
  BLUE (0,0,255)->(44,70,200), WHITE for prop bodies ->(236,232,220) so
  benches/horses stop blooming. Keep texture-face props unchanged.

  Validation: python3 port/world3d/tools/check_world.py; mesh byte deltas
  from port/world3d/generated/memory-budget.json; native 42-frame capture;
  rerun the mean-value script in section 4 and aim for 0.55-0.68 per room.

3.2 port/frontends/n64/adventure/scene.c + main.c - runtime contact shadows
  Add
    void scene_draw_shadow(float x,float z,float rx,float rz,float y);
  drawing an 8-vertex GL_TRIANGLE_FAN at y (0.02 in rooms, ground+0.02 on
  the island), glDisable(GL_TEXTURE_2D), glColor4ub(14,10,28,255), with
  the depth test on and depth write OFF (glDepthMask(GL_FALSE)) so a foot
  passing through it never z-fights; draw all shadows BEFORE the actors.
  The naming screen in naming.c already does exactly this with a 24-segment
  fan; reuse its parameters (0.78 x 0.60 at scale 1 looks right under the
  642-tri Mark; use 0.43 x 0.32 for the smaller fallback actors, i.e. scale
  by the per-actor `scale` argument of draw_presented_actor). Island: Mark
  at scale .48 and the cursor ball (0.55 scale, floating at 1.8) should get
  a shadow on the grass under the ball; that reads as "hovering" like the
  concept's Poke Ball with its soft ground shadow.
  Also call rdpq_mode_dithering(DITHER_SQUARE_SQUARE) (or the GL
  equivalent libdragon exposes) once after gl_init(): with 3.1's gradients
  in RGBA16 you will see banding on walls without ordered dither, and VI's
  dedither filter is designed to smooth exactly that pattern.
  Cost: 6 tris per visible actor; negligible RSP time.

3.3 build_world.py walls() - three-band wall
  Replace the single stretched plane with:
    wainscot  y 0..1.6  : wall_tex as now, uv v in [0,1] over 1.6 units
                           (so the 2-tile source band is 1:1), u repeating
                           every 2 tiles as now.
    upper     y 1.6..H  : untextured quad, colour = img.at(4,4) ("brown",
                           the source's outer wall colour) with wall_v
                           gradient from 3.1.
    cornice   y H-0.5..H: the source's decorated north row (crop
                           (16,8,32,8)) repeated; on the Lightning room
                           this is the hazard frieze and on the lobby the
                           gold moulding. Omit on rooms whose north row is
                           plain.
  The north wall keeps its full textured elevation plane (it carries the
  room's wall decoration) but gets the same vertical gradient. Keep the
  one-continuous-face-per-run structure (the comment about antialiased
  seams is right; do not go back to per-tile quads).
  Height: H=4.4 for standard rooms; keep Grass/Challenge Hall at 2 (they
  are gardens/stadiums) and Dome entrance at 3.5.

3.4 Floor textures (optional in pass 1, recommended in pass 2)
  Today Scene.floor() extracts 32x32 texels per 4x4 tiles = 8 texels per
  unit, i.e. 1:1 with the GBC pixels. That is correct resolution for the
  era; the problem is only content (neon grout, full value). For the three
  shared floors (lobby, foyer, lab/deck room) author a 32x32 "material"
  tile in port/maps/ or port/world3d/tools/ and reference it by slug in
  floor(): tile colour at 0.8 value, grout 1 texel at 0.65 value, and a
  2-level checker (+/-4%) across the 2x2-tile repeat so bilinear magnifies
  into soft variation instead of flat colour. Keep GL_REPEAT by emitting UVs
  in tile units.
  Pass 2: convert floor/wall textures to CI4 with `mksprite -f CI4` so a
  64x64 tile fits the 4 KiB TMEM budget at 2 KiB and doubles texel density
  for the near floor (the bottom third of every frame is the most
  magnified surface in the game). Verify libdragon preview GL honours CI4
  palettes through glSpriteTextureN64 before converting more than one.

  * fighting_club: the y=12 row of 10 'weight' props should be one rack:

  * water_club: 'horse' and 'bench' bodies to (236,232,220) with bottom

  * grass_club: topiary balls -> two-tone (upper half GREEN, lower half

3.6 port/world3d/tools/author_overworld.py - island
  * Sea: raise the water material toward cobalt (the concept's
    #1f5fd0-ish), brighten foam, and give the sea plane a very gentle
    gradient (0.9 near the island, 1.0 far) so the coast reads.
  * Shadows: for each building group emit a flat dark polygon offset
    (+0.25,+0.25) south-east at ground+0.012 (the SUN vector already in the
    file defines the direction); for conifers a small 6-gon under each.
    Cheap, baked, and exactly what the concept shows.
  * Beach: a 0.35-unit sand ring (cream (230,210,160)) along coast edges
    before the cliff faces; the concept's shoreline is the single feature
    that most says "N64 world map".
  * Cursor ball: shadow from 3.2.
  * Camera: fine. The concept's slightly lower angle (65-70 degrees) vs the
    current ~70 is not worth re-exporting the fixed-camera backface cull.

  * Damage flash "-40": keep, but outline the number.

3.8 dialogue.c, naming.c, extra_ui.c, main.c label - one UI language

------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. TRADE-OFFS AND RISKS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Vertex AO on coarse quads looks blotchy if the gradient crosses only
  one quad; hence the 2x2-tile subdivision near walls. Do not subdivide
  the whole floor.
* RGBA16 gradients band without dither; enable RDP dither when adding
  gradients, and remember VI dedither is on, so the final image will be
  smoother than the RDP captures.
* Darkening floors lowers readability of the walkable grid slightly; the
  aisle pool term compensates, and the player always has a shadow now.
* The ceiling slab sits at the near plane for the high camera in short
  rooms (eye y=7, wall 4.4): check the fov-64 rooms for near-plane clipping
  (near=1.5); if it clips, use the 5.5-unit wall option instead.
* Depth-write-off shadows draw in actor order; two adjacent actors'
  shadows overlap harmlessly because both are the same opaque colour.
* CI4 conversion is the only item with a real toolchain unknown; prove it
  on one texture first.
* None of this touches the story engine, saves, duel engine, NPC
  coordinates or collision; all geometry/UV/pivot data is preserved by
  design, and the approved Lightning mesh is only re-coloured.
