INDEPENDENT PRESENTATION REVIEW - Pokemon Trading Card Game port as an N64 game =============================================================================== Reviewer basis -------------- Inspected: captures/contact-sheet.png, every one of the 27 native PNGs in captures/ (320x240 RDP RGBA16, pre-VI), each also at 3x nearest-neighbour zoom, captures/manifest.json, the original room renders and briefs in port/maps/ (*.png, *.txt, 3D_GUIDE.md, club_lobby_3d.txt), and both concept images in port/maps/prototypes/. Source read for timing, lighting, camera and UI behaviour: port/world3d/tools/{build_world.py,room_style.py,scene_specs.py, author_overworld.py}, port/frontends/n64/adventure/{main,scene,presentation, dialogue,intro,naming}.c, port/frontends/n64/tcgn64.c, banner_font.h, and the web files listed in the brief (for parity only). Not done: I did not play the game, run an emulator, or see VI-filtered output. Every statement about motion, pacing or input feel below is marked "[code]" and is inferred from the listed source, not observed. Pixel-level comments account for the missing VI antialias/dedither pass: it will soften edges and dither grain, but it will not remove moire in dense floor patterns, fill missing geometry, or change value/colour hierarchy. Measured on the native PNGs (ImageMagick, 0..1 luminance / HSL saturation): map-01 (Lab) mean 0.59 sat 0.25 map-15 (Lightning) mean 0.44 sat 0.88 map-27 (Fire) mean 0.60 sat 0.50 map-32 (Dome) mean 0.49 sat 0.59 map-33 (Honor) mean 0.52 sat 0.47 map-00 (Island) mean 0.55 sat 0.69 duel.png mean 0.43 sat 0.47 These are only used to support what is visible: Lightning is far more saturated than anything else, and the duel board is the darkest screen. 1. SUMMARY VERDICT ------------------ The port already has the bones of a credible 1999 N64 adventure: fixed cameras per room, low-poly blocky people that read at 320x240, source-faithful layouts, a consistent cream-paper UI language shared by dialogue, naming and duel banners, and one room (Lightning Club) that is genuinely a "poster" room. What stops it reading as a finished product is not polygon count or texture size. It is four systemic things that every screenshot shares: A. A dark brown void above every wall, plus walls that fade to 43 percent at the top, so the upper 10-40 percent of every room shot reads as a cave ceiling or a hole. In the tutorial camera (mason-dialogue.png) it is about 40 percent of the frame. B. Floors carry source-resolution patterns that alias into moire at this camera distance (Fire checker, Dome carpet, Water parquet, Lightning ribs), while walls and props are low-detail flat boxes. The detail is in the wrong place for the hierarchy: the floor shouts, the focal props whisper. C. Nothing is grounded. Actor contact shadows are nearly invisible, props have only a faint baked darkening, the light-emitting props (braziers, torches, pool) emit nothing, so rooms with a strong mood in the source (Fire, Psychic, Hall of Honor, Water) are flat and cold. D. Everything textual uses the libdragon 8x8 system font with a 1px drop shadow, and duel feedback (coin, damage, piles) is text and rectangles. The one proportional anti-aliased face (the banner font) shows how much better the same screens could look. Fixing A and C is cheap (a handful of constants and two dozen triangles per room). B is content work on a few textures. D is a font asset plus a coin sprite. Together they would move the impression from "renderer demo of the GBC maps" to "small, charming N64 game", without changing any rules, positions, collision or rigs. 2. WHAT ALREADY WORKS (keep these) ---------------------------------- * Lightning Club (map-15.png). The indigo floor, cyan-white bolt, cream quadrant discs, magenta burst and orange badge pillars survive the trip to 3D and the composition (camera straight down the bolt, pillars framing the player) is the best in the set. It is the room to calibrate the others to. * Pokemon Dome (map-32.png). The raised purple dais with the yellow kerb, steps, green duel table and serpent statues reads as a stage. The grand scale works. * Club lobby (map-14.png) and Mason Laboratory (map-01.png). Source textures mapped on cabinet fronts, the orange counter, red chairs and green duel table give these the most "furnished" feel. Props with real front textures beat flat-colour primitives everywhere they appear. * Grass Club (map-18.png). Log fence, hedge, flower beds, topiaries and scattered stones read as a garden at a glance. * Characters. At this distance the big-head blocky rigs with textured faces read instantly; the player's red cap is findable in every room; white coats identify lab staff. Walk cycle ties stride to distance [code: step_actor in presentation.c uses a cycle per two tiles], run adds a 6 degree lean, idle has a tiny bob. All period-appropriate. * UI language. Cream paper (247,247,222), navy ink (24,48,80), blue edge (74,115,156) are used consistently by dialogue.c, naming.c, the duel banner and the island label. The speaker tag on the dialogue panel and the "A/B: continue" hint are clear. The naming screen (naming.png) is clean and complete. * Duel banner (duel-player-turn.png, duel-opponent-turn.png). The bevelled paper plaque with the anti-aliased proportional font is the most finished-looking element in the game. * Title fidelity (title.png). Logo, blink timing and copyright are faithful [code: intro.c draw_frame keeps the 60-tick delay and 37/26 blink]. * Camera system. One fixed camera per room, smooth 24 percent per-frame lerp when the cinematic camera changes [code: update_camera], a letterbox shutter into duels [code: adventure_duel_transition, 16 frames] and a 6-step tinted fade on warps [code: warp_fade] are all sensible N64 choices. 3. ART-DIRECTION TARGET ----------------------- One sentence: "A bright, toy-like card-club island, lit like a tabletop diorama, where each club room is a single strong colour idea seen from a fixed camera, and the UI is printed card stock." Concretely: * Value plan per room shot, top to bottom: ceiling band dark-mid (not black, not a hole), far wall mid and even, floor light but calm, focal prop brightest and most saturated, player and NPCs darker than the floor with a crisp shadow under each. Right now the floor is the brightest and busiest element in almost every room; the focal prop should win. * Palette: keep the source club theme colours (3D_GUIDE.md table) as the accent, desaturate the floor field to roughly 50-65 percent of source saturation, and keep walls neutral per room. Lightning should remain the most saturated room, but Fighting (map-06.png) and Lightning floors are currently beyond what a CRT shows cleanly in 16-bit. * Texture rule: no pattern period smaller than 2 tiles on floors, so that at the far wall a repeat is still 10-12 pixels wide. Detail budget goes to the front faces of focal props and to wall friezes at eye height, which the camera sees nearly square-on. * Light rule: every room has exactly one implied light direction (from the camera side, slightly from the left, matching the island SUN vector in author_overworld.py), expressed as baked vertex colour: prop tops 1.0, fronts 0.9, sides 0.75, backs 0.65. Emitters (braziers, torches, pool, screens) add a local warm or cool pool on the floor and on neighbouring wall faces. No runtime lights needed. * Shadow rule: one dark elliptical contact blob under every actor, visible on every floor; props get the same blob where they touch the floor. * UI rule: one typeface family. Headings and speaker names in the banner font; body text in a matching proportional 8px pixel font with the same ink colour; the system font kept only for debug. 4. HIGH-IMPACT FEASIBLE WORK, IN PRIORITY ORDER ----------------------------------------------- P1. Close the void above the walls and even out the far wall Evidence: map-01, map-06, map-09, map-12, map-18, map-21, map-24, map-27, map-32, map-33 all show a dark brown band across the top of frame; mason-dialogue.png and sam-table.png show it taking roughly 40 percent of the frame; map-18.png shows it over a garden fence where the brief asks for bright sky; map-32.png shows a black slit where the open north door gap exposes it. Cause [code]: main.c game_render_frame clears to (26,18,14) for every interior; walls() in build_world.py builds 5-unit walls with nothing above them; bake_lighting in room_style.py darkens side and north wall vertices from 0.86 at the floor to 0.43 at y>=5 and the south wall to a flat 0.43; the tutorial tracking camera in presentation.c update_camera (eye = Mark + (4.5, 6.8, 10.5), target y=1, fov 52) pitches down only about 25 degrees, so with a 52 degree vertical fov the top of frame is above the horizon. Fix: a. Add a ceiling plane per room at wall height (two triangles, flat colour = wall paint at 35-45 percent value), or a 1.5-unit cornice soffit box along the north and side walls. This alone turns the hole into a ceiling. b. Raise the wall top value from 0.43 to about 0.70 and keep a gentle gradient (0.95 at floor to 0.70 at top); keep the south low wall at about 0.6 so it still frames the bottom edge. c. Set the clear colour per room to the ceiling colour instead of a global brown, so any remaining sliver matches. d. Grass Club only: clear to a pale sky (e.g. 150,200,235) and give the hedge strip a lighter top, matching grass_club.txt ("let the sky be bright"). e. Behind every wall door gap draw a dark doorway quad (door panel texture from the source north band, or a 0.3-value plane set 0.5 units back) so open doors read as doors, not holes (map-32.png). f. Tutorial camera: lower the target y from 1 to about 0.2 and raise the eye to about 8.5 (or reduce fov to 46) so the top of frame stays at or below the wall top while Mark runs; verify on the run toward Mason and the Sam framing (eye center+(.5,10,10) is fine). Cost: small. Content: none. Validation: re-capture; the mean luminance of rows 0-29 in map-01/map-21 should rise from about 0.48 to above 0.55 and the top band should sample a single flat colour, not 26,18,14. P2. Calm the floors, move detail to walls and focal props Evidence: map-27.png (Fire) cream/orange cross-hatch checker shimmers at the far half; map-32.png (Dome) red diamond carpet is moire across the bottom half; map-12.png (Water) orange-lined parquet is noisy everywhere; map-15.png indigo ribs and map-06.png bright green grid vibrate; map-09 (Rock) speckled strip flickers. Dense floor pattern plus flat side faces on props inverts the hierarchy. Cause [code]: Scene.floor in build_world.py bakes source pixels at 1 source pixel = 1 texel, one 32x32 texture per 4x4 tiles (8 texels per tile) with no mip levels; scene.c requests GL_LINEAR only. At the far wall a tile is 5-7 pixels, so 1-tile motifs alias no matter what VI does. Fix: a. Author 2-tile-period variants of the four worst floors (Fire checker: one cream and one warm cross-hatch tile each 2x2; Dome carpet: one diamond per 2 tiles; Water parquet: 2-tile boards; Lightning: rib every 2 tiles with the cyan micro-highlight dropped or made every 4). Keep the exact source colours; only the period changes. b. Generate a 16x16 mip for each floor texture (box-filtered) and enable it in scene_open (libdragon GL supports mipmapped sprites); if that is not available in this build, pre-blur the far-field half of each floor texture in build_world.floor using the tile's distance from the camera, which the fixed camera makes legal. c. Reduce floor saturation in room_style.FLOOR_TINT for fighting_club (currently absent, so 0.85 neutral) and lightning_club (0.97) to a tinted 0.80, and desaturate the Fighting green by mixing 15 percent towards the wall paint in material_pixel. d. Give prop boxes per-face shading in Scene.box (top 1.0, front 0.9, sides 0.75, back 0.65) so brown cabinets and white machines stop reading as untextured cubes (map-01, map-24, map-12 benches). Cost: a few hours of texture authoring plus small code changes. No layout change. Validation: zoom 3x on the far third of each fixed floor; no repeat should be narrower than 10 pixels; count unique colours per frame (result.json "colors") should fall slightly, not rise. P3. Ground everything: shadows and emitter glow Evidence: in map-01.png, map-15.png and map-21.png actors have no visible shadow; topiaries (map-18), braziers (map-27), torches (map-21), statues (map-09) appear to float; the six Fire braziers and eight Psychic torches light nothing; the Hall of Honor altar has no spotlight; the Water pool has no highlight or reflection. Cause [code]: scene_draw_contact_shadows draws a fan with centre alpha 64 fading to 0 at a 0.56 x 0.40 radius, which on pale floors averages a few percent darkening; bake_lighting applies prop contact of strength 0.24 (0.10 for tables/chairs) with a 0.7-unit gaussian, and torch/brazier glow of only 0.15 in the floor tint colour (not warm). Fix: a. Actor blob: centre alpha 64 -> 120, add a second inner fan at radius 0.32 with alpha 90, keep rim 0. Keep it on the floor plane (already independent of bob). This is the single cheapest readability win. b. Prop grounding: raise contact strength to 0.40 for solid props and widen the gaussian to 1.0 unit; also emit the same ellipse fan under every prop footprint at build time (two triangles each, baked into the floor batch as a dark vertex-coloured decal) so tables and pots sit down. c. Emitter glow: for 'brazier' and 'torch' bake a warm pool (Fire: +0.35 red, +0.18 green over a 2.2-unit gaussian; Psychic: violet pool) into the floor vertices and the nearest wall band, and tint the flame cones with a 2-ring vertical gradient (white core at the base, saturated tip) so they stop reading as traffic cones. d. Hall of Honor: the existing focal boost (+0.22 at 12,12) is too subtle to see in map-33.png; raise it to +0.45 and narrow it to the altar footprint, then darken the room's perimeter floor by 0.15 so the altar is the brightest thing in frame. e. Water pool: add a second scrolling layer, or simply a lighter rim band and a 2-frame alternating glint texture [code: swimming_pool already has a wave texture; animate its UV by one texel every 4 frames at 30Hz via a second batch toggled in scene_draw_room]. Also fix the jagged diagonal pool edge visible in map-12.png (pool_rows steps 1 tile per row; use 2 rows per step or chamfer). Cost: small code, no content. Validation: in each room, sample the floor luminance 0.3 units in front of an NPC vs 2 units away; expect a 20-30 percent drop. Fire floor near a brazier should be measurably warmer (R-B difference up) than mid-room. P4. Island: scale, surface and sea Evidence: map-00.png vs overworld_n64_concept_v1.png. The native island is a flat, uniformly saturated green plate; buildings are 10-14 pixel white blobs with coloured lids (the concept prompt explicitly warned against this); the conifer forest is a visible grid of identical blobs; the sea is flat blue with faint diagonal streaks; loose brown rocks float at the bottom right; the volcano is a smooth brown cone. Cause [code]: author_overworld.py hall() builds halls 1.25 x 0.95 units (the source icon is 2 x 2 tiles), with STONE walls that run to (239, 231,202) and TRIM at (239,233,208), so facades blow out to white at this size; the native island camera is eye (10,22,24.6), target (10,.25,8.8), fov 38 (about 54 degrees down), so the door, window and lintel detail front() builds is 2-3 pixels tall; trees are planted on a 0.64 x 0.65 grid with +-0.13 jitter; the water texture is a 64x32 noise with sparse wave highlights and no shoreline band. Fix: a. Scale every landmark hall by 1.35-1.5 (keep node positions; they are the source travel coordinates) and lower STONE high end to about (215,205,180) so walls are stone, not paper. Roofs keep their colours; make the red Dome roof and the lab's blue roof the two brightest. b. Double the window and door sizes in front() so each is at least 4 pixels wide at native; add one dark eave line under every roof edge (TRIM at 0.7 value) so roofs separate from walls. c. Trees: raise jitter to +-0.28 and drop rate to 0.25, add a second, darker pine material for every third tree, and push the crown shade range (currently 0.86/1.0/1.1) to 0.75/1.0/1.2. d. Sea: add a 0.35-unit shoreline foam band (light quad strip along all_land outlines at SEA+0.02, 60 percent white) and a one-texel UV scroll of the water texture every 3 frames [code: would need a second batch or a texture-matrix update in game_render_frame]. Even a 2-phase alternation reads as water on a CRT. e. Remove or sink the detached rocks at the bottom right unless they are the source's sea rocks; if they are, give them a foam ring. f. Grass: the current texture is one green; blend two (sunlit and shaded) by the baked grounding factor and add the dirt path's soft edge (path() already exists; widen its blend by 0.1). Cost: moderate content work in Blender script; camera unchanged. Validation: at 3x zoom, every building's door should be visible; the coast should read as a line of foam, not a hard edge; the forest should not show rows. P5. One typeface and real duel feedback Evidence: every text screen (mason-dialogue, sam-table, naming, all duel-*.png) uses the heavy 8x8 system font with a 1px shadow; it is legible but looks like a debug overlay next to the banner font in duel-player-turn.png. duel-coin.png shows the coin toss as a text panel ("Coin toss / A: toss") and show_frames prints HEADS/TAILS; damage is a red rectangle with "-20" (duel.png); prizes are 5x7 squares; deck and discard are numbers only. Cause [code]: dialogue.c print and tcgn64.c print use graphics_draw_text; banner_text has its own 4-bit coverage font (banner_font.h); TCG_EV_COIN runs show_frames(20) with no sprite; TCG_EV_DAMAGE sets g_dmg.timer=40 and draws fill+damage_number with no flash or offset. Fix: a. Add a proportional 8px body font in the same coverage format as banner_font.h (uppercase, lowercase, digits, punctuation, the source's male/female and energy glyphs). Use it in dialogue.c draw_panel, naming.c draw_keyboard and the duel text box / card page. Keep wrap() metrics by measuring advances. Letter shapes should match the banner font so the plaque and the text box feel like one print job. b. Coin: draw a 16x16 coin sprite (heads/tails from the source sprites) in the coin panel; on TCG_EV_COIN run a 12-frame flip (scale x by cos over 6 frames, swap face at the narrowest frame, then 8 frames of rest) before the result text. c. Damage: for the first 6 frames of g_dmg.timer invert the art rectangle to white for 2 frames and offset the slot by +-2 px (mirrors the web 'hit' animation in style.css), then show the red number. Add a 4-frame bar drain on hp_bar instead of a jump. d. Piles: draw deck and discard as small card-back rectangles (C_CARDBACK with a 1px light edge) with the count on them in the info bar, and prizes as 6 tiny card-backs that grey out when taken. e. Bench slots: empty slots as a 1px dotted outline in C_MID rather than a solid rectangle, so filled slots pop. Cost: font asset is the main work; the rest is small. Validation: compare the dialogue panel and banner side by side at 3x; the two faces should share stroke weight. Coin and damage can be checked with the existing automated duel capture by adding a frame dump at timer 38 and timer 20. P6. TRANSITIONS, PACING AND FEEDBACK [code only] ----------------------------------------------- * warp_fade: 6 frames in, 6 out at 30Hz (0.2 s each) to the room clear colour. Brisk; it may read as a hard cut after VI. Consider 8 frames with a 4-frame hold at full, and fade to the P1 ceiling colour. * adventure_duel_transition: 16-frame letterbox shutter, eased (quadratic); good. Consider a 1-frame white flash at full close. * Camera lerp 0.24 per frame: 95 percent in about 11 frames (0.37 s). Fine. * Walking: 8 rendered frames per tile at walk, 4 at run; tutorial motion at 70 percent speed with a per-actor remainder. Reasonable; the bob of 0.035 units is subtle enough. * Duel pacing: show_frames(20) after coin and damage (0.67 s), WAIT capped at 30 frames, A/B skips. Fine once the coin and hit visuals exist; without them 20 frames of a static panel feels like a stall. * Dialogue: per-character reveal paced by text speed; A/B advances. Good. * Denied input: a 3px red frame around the whole screen for g_denied frames is loud; a 2-frame shake of the cursor or a dimmed option would be gentler and more period-typical. 7. OPTIONAL EXPERIMENTS (do after P1-P5, only if cheap) ------------------------------------------------------- * A 2-frame "breathing" vertex-colour pulse on brazier/torch glow (toggle between two baked floor batches every 8 frames). * A faint 32x32 vignette quad in screen space (alpha 0.18 at corners) over every room to pull the eye to centre; N64 games used this via the framebuffer blend. Check it does not crush the P1 ceiling band. * Per-room fog colour set to the ceiling colour with a far start, to soften the far wall very slightly (RDP fog is free). * Spotlight discs: the Lightning floor discs read as lights; add a 20 percent brighter cone of floor vertices above them to imply spots. * NPC head turn toward the player when spoken to (yaw the head mesh part by up to 30 degrees; rigs unchanged). * Interaction cue: a small "!" sprite above an NPC when facing them within one tile. * Island: smoke sprite from the volcano (the source draws it) and a 2-frame boat bob. * Title: a slow horizontal scroll of a repeating card-back texture behind the logo instead of the flat cream. 8.
