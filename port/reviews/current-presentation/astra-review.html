Astra High

PRESENTATION REVIEW — NINTENDO 64 TARGET Evidence and limits I inspected captures/contact-sheet.png first, then all 27 individual PNGs and captures/manifest.json. PNG inspection worked. I judged the images at native 320x240 and inspected nearest-neighbor enlargements of the Lightning and Rock clubs, Hall of Honor, dialogue, duel actions and card details. I also inspected the two supplied concept images and relevant original room images/descriptions, and read presentation-relevant portions of the explicitly listed sources. I did not play the game, launch an emulator, inspect final VI scanout, or use project history or other reviews. Movement and timing observations below are explicitly code-based. No production files were changed. The captures are 16-bit RDP output before VI filtering. Some fine stippling, stair-stepping and narrow edge noise may soften considerably on the intended display. These images do establish large-scale composition, competing values, prop silhouettes and interface hierarchy. They cannot establish shimmer, frame pacing, input responsiveness, sound quality or final text legibility. Recommendations assume 320x240, 30 Hz and 4 MiB, not a modern rendering budget. Overall assessment and art direction The port has a convincing foundation for a colorful N64 game, but it currently looks unevenly authored. The island, naming screen and tutorial dialogue feel deliberate. Several club interiors feel like a source floor image laid beneath a kit of primitives. The duel is readable when studied, yet presents too much information with the same small-text emphasis. More polygons everywhere would not address the largest problems. Use a coherent target: a miniature island of painted club interiors, populated by readable toy-like characters, with card-table interfaces in cream, navy and muted green. Preserve each club's original layout, color identity and motifs. Make broad fields quiet, the room's signature object distinct, and characters and current actions the clearest small shapes. Painted highlights, broad vertex gradients, small filtered textures and restrained contact shadows suit this direction. Do not aim for glossy realistic surfaces, bloom or tiny ornament. The Lightning concept is useful for its hierarchy: quiet blue floor, readable figures, grounded pillars and a bright destination. The native map-15.png has many of the same ingredients, but the dark floor grid and pillar texture carry more visual weight than the people. Transfer that hierarchy, not the concept's resolution or rendering richness. The overworld concept similarly demonstrates distinct landmarks and cohesive land/water materials; map-00.png already gets much of its miniature-island appeal. Qualities to retain * map-00.png has a strong island silhouette, legible coastline, distinct roof colors, bridges and a pleasing forest/volcano contrast. It promises a place worth exploring. The small cream destination label is useful and restrained. * naming.png is one of the most complete compositions: the model preview, keyboard, name slots and controls each have a clear place. The existing character rig reads well at this larger presentation scale. * mason-dialogue.png and sam-table.png keep the speaker and player in view and use a clear cream dialogue box with a separate speaker tab. The restrained tile treatment in map-01.png and map-14.png supports characters much better than the loud patterned floors elsewhere. * Clubs are identifiable from color and arrangement. Water's asymmetric pool, Grass's low enclosing wall and planting, Lightning's central inlay and the Dome's stepped outline are valuable distinctive forms. * The green felt, original card illustrations, HP bars and highlighted current player provide a workable duel vocabulary. The cream turn plaques in duel-player-turn.png and duel-opponent-turn.png are clearer focal points than the surrounding small text. Preserve that useful contrast. HIGH-IMPACT, FEASIBLE WORK — IN PRIORITY ORDER 1. Re-author floor and prop texture contrast for the actual camera scale. Evidence: map-12.png's dense orange deck grid competes with the pool and white benches; map-27.png's repeated orange floor pattern overwhelms its six small braziers; map-06.png's green weave is more conspicuous than the gym kit; map-09.png combines blue grout, speckled gold strips and a bright red runner; map-32.png's foreground carpet dominates the central duel table. map-15.png's near-black grout makes the blue floor a cage of lines. The red runners shared by several rooms also compete directly with the player's red cap and jacket. This is a design issue even if VI filtering removes some pixel noise. Reduce local contrast and motif density on supporting surfaces while retaining their identity. Start with Fire, Water and the Dome: preserve orange/yellow deck colors, red/gold ceremonial carpet and room borders, but simplify interior marks and make grout a nearby value instead of a hard dark or bright line. Keep one clearly readable pattern scale per material. Enlarge or simplify a repeating motif inside the existing material footprint rather than stretching the entire original room image. Reserve the brightest cream/yellow accents for steps, important inlays and focal furniture. Make the runner red deeper so the player separates from it. Do not uniformly desaturate all rooms into beige. Source: build_world.py Scene.floor() selects source patches and emits 32x32 texture chunks; room_style.py material_pixel(), TILES and FLOOR_TINT already provide a suitable per-room authoring seam. The lab/lobby replacement flagstones demonstrate that selective reinterpretation works. The generic fallback's 12% luminance blend cannot remove dense source motifs. Lightning is imported by copy_lightning(), so do not assume all floor changes flow through Scene.floor(). Cost/validation: primarily replace existing small textures and colors; avoid adding layers or more unique textures by default. Compare before/after at 1x, in grayscale, and with actual VI output later. At a glance the player, room subject and exit should win over the floor grid. Preserve motif boundaries, walkable areas and all source coordinates. Test a short walk across patterned areas for shimmer on the target; no shimmer claim is made from these stills. 2. Give repeated props specific silhouettes and properly authored faces. Evidence: map-09.png's boulders read as broad brown loaves and its gold statues as generic round-headed posts; map-27.png and map-21.png's flame heads resemble cones on sticks; map-18.png's topiary repeats nearly identical rounded masses. map-24.png's machines are long white-topped slabs whose detail is mostly on the front face. In map-33.png the intended Poké Ball is largely a dark cap behind a wide white slab, while the altar front is a compressed blue/white image. That weakens the climactic room more than a lack of surface effects does. Spend the modeling pass on a small set of recognizable recipes, reused at the existing positions. Give rocks uneven angular profiles and a few broad shaded faces; give statues a clear head/torso/base and one recognizable outline feature; give braziers a visible bowl and a deliberately shaped two- or three-part flame; give machines an inset screen and a readable top/control-plane separation. For the Hall, expose a properly rounded ball silhouette with a broad band and button, and separate the altar's top, riser and column faces. Inspect its vertical placement against the slab before adding ornament. Restore essential original wall focal motifs where absent or too small, particularly the Fighting sign and Hall banner. These are existing identity features, not new UI text. Source: build_world.py prop() constructs the recipes. It crops the complete source prop image for a front or tabletop texture; that source image can already contain perspective and shading, so mapping it wholesale can bake the wrong surface cues into a 3D object. Author separate top/front/side patches from the same palette. Its ball branch fixes the center at y=2.5, while the altar adds a raised slab; inspect this overlap for the cap-like appearance. scene_specs.py PROPS supplies stable placements, footprints and heights. walls() deliberately uses south-wall livery, which avoids duplicated cabinetry but does not by itself reconstruct unique north-wall signs. Compare with the supplied room PNGs and descriptions before selecting which unique features to recover. Keep character rigs, collision and story outcomes unchanged. Most of this can be done by redistributing the polygons already used by balls/cylinders and by reusing small material patches. Validate each replacement as a silhouette and at its smallest native camera size; if the new feature disappears at 1x, remove it. Check screen-space occlusion around nearby NPCs and unchanged source paths. Prioritize Hall, Rock and Fire before minor furnishings. 3. Tune per-room cameras and focal composition around people and destinations. Evidence: map-01.png has a very large tiled foreground and tiny figures at the back. map-15.png's far opponent is dwarfed by the bright shrine and pillars; map-21.png and map-27.png place most important figures near the far edge of a long corridor. map-24.png devotes much of the picture to the purple floor. The close tutorial compositions in mason-dialogue.png and sam-table.png show how much better the existing people read when given sufficient pixels. Author a small table of camera overrides rather than asking one proportional camera formula to finish every room. Adjust eye height, target and FOV together to reduce unused foreground and increase the far NPC's apparent size while retaining the entry, navigable side lanes and room identity. For long rooms, prototype a restrained presentation-only conversation framing that keeps both participants above the dialogue box; a single permanent zoom can crop side paths and is not automatically an improvement. Preserve the useful fixed-room overview where it works. Do not move NPCs or change their rigs to solve framing. Source: build_world.py camera() mostly derives its view from room dimensions, with a small number of exceptions. presentation.c update_camera() already has Mason/Sam framing and smooths eye, target and FOV by 0.24 per rendered frame. dialogue.c reserves the bottom 80 pixels for its panel. Tune toward that actual safe region, not an unobstructed editor viewport. main.c game_render_frame() draws the world with the presentation camera. Validation: capture the entrance, every conversation position, side exits and both far corners, with and without dialogue. Check faces, feet and navigation landmarks at 320x240. Inspect the complete approach to Mason and Sam in motion for framing changes and line-of-sight occlusion. Camera changes alone cost little, but a closer view may increase overdraw; measure the busiest view. 4. Make lighting and contact communicate material and depth consistently. Evidence: broad wall gradients already help map-01.png and map-14.png, and small character contacts are visible. Nevertheless, large objects in map-09.png, map-18.png and map-24.png can read as separate primitives sitting on a printed floor. Fire's floor remains broadly bright while its apparent light sources are small and pale; Psychic has a distinct purple palette but little sense of localized light. Hall's broad white top competes with the object it should display. Do not describe these rooms as having no shadows: contacts and baked shading already exist. Choose a consistent broad key direction per room and use it on boxes, rounded props and their short grounding shadows. Keep soft ambient light sufficient for faces and routes. Strengthen localized floor darkening just under large props and table legs before darkening entire rooms. Differentiate materials through broad painted response: quiet matte floor, darker wood side faces, limited pale machine edges, and gold accents with a small bright face. In Fire, paint restrained warm pools around the braziers and keep the enclosing walls cooler; in Psychic use subdued violet pools and stronger silhouette contrast. These are baked cues, not a request for runtime point lights. Source: room_style.py bake_lighting() already applies perimeter darkening, prop proximity, aisle/focal boosts and torch glow to environment vertices. Scene.floor() provides only a few samples per chunk; a narrow contact field can fall between vertices. Check sampling before merely increasing darkness, and add a few local samples only where necessary. build_world.py Scene.box(), cylinder() and ball() use different hard-coded shade recipes; align their visual key direction. scene.c scene_draw_contact_shadows() already supplies a soft 12-segment fan per character, independent of bob. Retain its modest footprint. author_overworld.py mesh(), grounding_factor() and bake_ground_lighting() show an existing directional/grounding approach; keep the island's gentle light. Validation: use one near and one far prop in each material family. Check that contacts touch the bases without making black puddles or climbing walls, and that shaded faces remain distinct after 16-bit quantization and VI filtering. Do not increase room-wide geometry density or translucency just to improve a small contact. Compare triangle, material-batch and resident-memory counts. 5. Finish the duel's information hierarchy and connect it to the adventure UI. Evidence: duel-action-menu.png is functional but compresses six actions, question text and shortcuts into four tightly packed lines. Both active cards' statistics use nearly the same weight as deck/hand/discard counts, while card art sits detached at the right. The bright turn plaques are clear but sit across part of the player's bench/count region in duel-player-turn.png and duel-opponent-turn.png. duel-card-list.png gives a large empty panel to a short hand and squeezes card identity/attack information into a narrow strip. duel-card-detail.png has abundant space but concentrates almost everything at the top, and leaves repeated prior rejection messages in the bottom log. duel-coin.png shows an instructional popup, not a visible coin at that captured moment. duel.png demonstrates useful damage/HP feedback, but it remains small. Keep the two-sided board and complete game information. Establish one strong active-Pokémon grouping per side: name, HP, energy and art should visibly belong together. De-emphasize empty bench outlines and supporting counts instead of adding badges. Retain cream/navy panels and a consistent selection treatment across dialogue, naming, lists and duel prompts. Use the larger banner face for brief headings only; dense rules still need compact, carefully spaced type. Use the existing banner's clarity as a hierarchy reference, not a requirement to enlarge every label. Give actions more vertical room while the action menu is open, with consistent column spacing and a distinct current selection. Place turn/coin prompts in a reserved area that does not conceal live card information, or temporarily compose the board around the prompt. Give card detail pages a deliberate header/art/attack structure and a clear Back hint, rather than filling empty space with prose. Prioritize relevant current guidance over repeated stale errors in the visible log, while retaining required engine acknowledgements. Test long attack text before enlarging the illustration or typography. Replace ambiguous single-letter energy shorthand with small recognizable symbols where they survive the native pixel budget; provide names on the detail page. Source: tcgn64.c draw_side(), draw_board(), draw_felt(), ui_duel_menu(), draw_card_list_screen(), draw_card_summary(), draw_card_page(), draw_textbox_hint(), ui_ack() and draw_duel_banner(). banner_font.h supplies an existing 18-pixel glyph mask resource. dialogue.c draw_panel() and naming.c draw_keyboard() provide the cream/blue counterpart. Web duel_banners.js and style.css already share this palette, but their resolution-independent CSS, font sizing and centered overlays are not evidence that the native layout is finished. Tune each renderer against the same hierarchy, not CSS pixel parity. Validation: review a full bench, long names, many energy attachments, low HP, statuses, long two-attack rules, trainer text and multi-coin results. Check the same states with banners and menus open. Preserve all engine answers, outcomes and input mappings. Test on filtered 320x240 output at normal viewing distance; require that active card, HP, selection and next available input can be found quickly. No high-resolution text dependency or new font family is needed. OPENING, NAMING, DIALOGUE AND TIMING title.png, intro-charizard.png, intro-scyther.png and intro-aerodactyl.png preserve appealing original artwork, but their flat pale field and small pack illustrations feel like a handheld opening beside the later 3D world. This is a coherence opportunity, not a broken screen. First carry the same cream/navy palette and disciplined spacing through the interface. A modest physical card/pack presentation is an optional later experiment; replacing the established opening should not precede the rooms and duel. Keep the recognizable logo and source sequence. intro.c draw_frame() scales a 160x144 composition by 1.5 and deliberately separates filtering for art and small lettering; judge that choice on actual VI output before changing filtering or redrawing all pixel artwork. One concrete naming polish issue: naming.png says "START: End", but naming.c adventure_name_player() uses Start to select the End item; A performs choose() and exits. Either make the hint accurately describe selection or make the direct End action match the hint after checking intended behavior. This is a presentation/input-expectation issue, not a request to alter naming rules. Dialogue boxes are already good. Preserve their modest borders and high contrast. The four-line layout uses ten-pixel line spacing and a separate bottom hint, so long dialogue and large speaker names should be checked for collisions, but the supplied Mason/Sam frames show no obvious overflow. Code-based timing findings, not observed motion: * presentation.c player_duration() gives ordinary movement eight rendered frames and running four: about 267 ms and 133 ms at 30 Hz. step_actor() ties gait to distance, limits heading changes to 45 degrees per frame, and uses a small idle bob. These are sensible restrained ingredients. Do not replace them with exaggerated squash, camera shake or continuous idle activity. * The tutorial scales presentation movement to 70% speed, preserving fractional remainder. update_camera() eases toward its target every render frame. Review the actual tutorial for camera lag, abrupt composition changes and whether the slower approach remains purposeful; the screenshots cannot decide this. * main.c warp_fade() has six frames per half, nominally 0.2 seconds each, plus room loading. dialogue.c adventure_duel_transition() uses sixteen frames per shutter sequence, nominally 0.53 seconds. These are reasonable starting lengths. Check actual load stalls and input carryover in a later runtime pass. * dialogue.c reveal() offers 1/2/3/6 characters per frame or instant text, and advance() first completes the current page. At the default speed the nominal rate is 90 characters/second. Keep this user control; verify sounds and response with real input rather than assuming the reveal feels slow or fast. * intro.c advances original animation time by two ticks per rendered frame. adventure_intro_play() requires title time >=60 ticks for confirmation, nominally a one-second title gate even after skipping the opening. Review the gate as a first-use responsiveness issue; the still title cannot establish it. * tcgn64.c on_event() presents coin results for 20 frames and damage for 20 frames with a 40-frame damage timer, nominally 0.67/1.33 seconds. show_frames() allows A/B to shorten waits. In the inspected native event handler there is no general animation-event case equivalent to web duel_ui.js onAnim(), which implements coin spin, shuffle and hit feedback. Native feedback should not be credited with those web effects. ui_ack() waits for acknowledgment; turn plaques should not be described as automatically timed banners. OPTIONAL EXPERIMENTS — AFTER THE FIVE PRIORITIES 1. Add very cheap native coin anticipation/result motion and a brief targeted hit pulse or HP interpolation. Use the existing engine event outcome, never visual randomness that changes results. Keep timing bounded, skippable where appropriate, and consistent with the disabled-animation option. A few small sprite frames or a scaled quad suffice; no 3D battle simulation is needed. 2. Animate only one environmental cue per relevant room: a small flame change, a very slow water highlight, or subtle light modulation. Keep it in existing batches where possible. Avoid a room full of moving textures and particles. 3. Try a simple card/pack tilt and short settling motion in the opening to link original illustrations with the miniature world. Compare against retaining the current sequence; do not assume more animation is an improvement. Practical acceptance sequence First make a small style proof in Fire, Rock, Hall and the duel detail/action screens. These expose the material, silhouette, composition and hierarchy problems with relatively little asset coverage. Compare each against its exact supplied screenshot, the original room image and the proposed art direction. Then propagate successful material and prop recipes to the other rooms. For the next review, recapture the same complete 27-image set, plus full benches, long card text and representative room conversation positions. Evaluate 1x native images and final VI output separately; retain zooms for diagnosing texture and geometry, not as the primary aesthetic test. Review short motion recordings of the title skip, naming, Mason/Sam sequence, room warps and a duel containing coin, damage, knockout and turn changes. Include audio and controller response in that later pass; neither was established here. Measure worst-view frame time against a 33.3 ms budget and peak allocation against the complete 4 MiB budget, including framebuffer/depth surfaces, texture residency, transition snapshots and duel buffers. Prefer texture replacement, shared prop recipes and vertex colors over extra passes. Confirm the original positions, collision, scripts, rigs and duel outcomes remain intact. The release impression should improve through clearer authored choices, not added UI copy or an unbounded list of effects.
