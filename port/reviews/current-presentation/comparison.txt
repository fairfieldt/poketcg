Independent presentation reviews

Fable 5.1 High (Claude Code) and GPT-6 Astra High received the identical brief
and the same 27 native screenshots. Neither received the development conversation
or earlier feedback. Both inspected the complete bundle and scoped source.
Their reports are preserved without editorial changes in fable-review.txt and
astra-review.txt. reviewers.json records the model settings and input hashes.

Shared priorities

1. Quiet supporting floor patterns, especially Fire, Water, Dome and Lightning.
   Preserve club colors/motifs while reducing contrast and small repeated marks
   so characters and focal objects win at 320x240.
2. Improve a few distinctive prop recipes rather than adding detail everywhere.
   Hall's altar/display, Rock's statues/boulders and Fire/Psychic flames are
   useful first targets. Preserve source placements, paths and rigs.
3. Compose each room around people and destinations. Both want less unused
   foreground/upper space and better far-character readability. Conversation
   framing should be tested with the actual bottom dialogue panel.
4. Strengthen material and light hierarchy. Tune existing baked shading and
   grounding locally, give emitters a deliberate warm/violet character, and
   make important objects brighter than their surrounding carpet.
5. Improve native duel hierarchy and feedback: group active card/name/HP/art,
   reserve space for prompts, simplify supporting counts and empty slots, and
   add readable coin/damage feedback without changing outcomes.

Meaningful differences

Fable's first priority is the dark area above walls and the far-wall gradient.
Astra puts floor content and recognizable silhouettes first, then room cameras.
Fable proposes a broader island treatment with larger landmarks and more sea/
forest variation. Astra regards the island as a strong asset to protect and
suggests only selective separation between paths, grass and pale buildings.
Fable favors a proportional body font matching the new banners. Astra prefers
retaining compact rules text and fixing layout/spacing before changing fonts.
Fable wants substantially denser actor shadows; Astra explicitly recommends
retaining modest contacts and checking sampling before adding darkness/passes.

Parent assessment and factual qualifications

The strongest shared direction is selective content authoring: calmer patterns,
recognizable focal props and better framing. A focused Fire/Rock/Hall proof and
a duel layout proof would establish the style before propagating it globally.
The island and existing compact text should be evaluated separately rather
than changed solely to reconcile two different preferences.

Some recommendations describe systems that already exist: textured box faces
receive per-face shading; indoor people have soft contact fans; floors have
prop proximity and emitter boosts; the island has grounding and shoreline foam.
The actionable issue is how strongly these read in the captures, not their
absence. Fable acknowledges several of these implementations later in its text.

An opaque full ceiling plane can obscure these rooms from their elevated
cameras. Soffits, backdrops, corner completion or camera adjustments should be
prototyped with actor/path visibility before adopting that specific suggestion.
Likewise, mipmaps or extra translucent/animated layers need a small toolchain
and frame-time prototype before broad changes.

Both reports are based on static pre-VI frames and source inspection. Claims
about shimmer/flicker, movement feel or the benefit of longer transitions remain
tentative until filtered moving output is observed. Numeric brightness targets
are aesthetic experiments, not hard correctness requirements.

One concrete native input issue identified by Astra is separate from the web
intro Start fix: naming's START:End hint currently selects End, then requires A.
The reports document that follow-up; the current review does not establish a
manual full-game or physical-console playthrough.
